# AO-001 – HELIX Organizational Ontology

Status: Architectural Candidate

Version: 0.1

Phase: II – Organizational Ontology

Classification: Reference Architecture

---

# Purpose

Define the fundamental concepts that exist in every organization.

The Organizational Ontology provides the common language upon which all HELIX reference models, specifications, standards and capabilities are built.

This document intentionally avoids implementation concerns and instead defines organizational reality at the highest architectural level.

---

# Scope

This ontology applies to every organization regardless of:

- Industry
- Size
- Legal structure
- Technology
- Human or AI workforce
- Geographic location

The concepts defined herein are considered implementation independent.

---

# Design Principles

The Organizational Ontology is governed by the following principles.

- Reality precedes architecture.
- Concepts are discovered rather than invented.
- Every concept must be independently observable.
- Every concept must be falsifiable.
- Every concept must possess a unique architectural responsibility.
- No concept may overlap another concept.
- Simplicity takes precedence over completeness.

---

# Fundamental Organizational Concepts

## Purpose

Purpose defines why an organization exists.

Purpose provides direction for all organizational activity and serves as the highest intentional concept within the organization.

---

## Organizational Capability

An Organizational Capability is a governed organizational ability that enables Actors to transform Organizational Objects in pursuit of Organizational Purpose.

Capabilities belong to the organization.

Actors exercise capabilities.

Capabilities persist independently of individual Actors.

---

## Organizational Actor

An Organizational Actor is a bearer of organizational responsibility capable of exercising one or more Organizational Capabilities.

Examples include:

- Human
- AI Employee
- Organizational Unit
- External Organization
- Robot
- Autonomous Software Agent

Actor describes a role within the organization rather than an implementation.

---

## Organizational Object

An Organizational Object is a governed organizational entity possessing identity, lifecycle, relationships and organizational meaning.

Organizational Objects represent the primary entities managed by the organization.

Two primary specializations have currently been identified.

---

## Organizational Purpose

Definition

The enduring justification for an organization's existence.

Responsibility

Explains why the organization exists.

---

## Organizational Relationship

Status

Validated

Definition

A persistent structural connection between organizational concepts that carries information not contained within the connected concepts themselves.

Responsibility

Provides structural meaning by connecting organizational concepts into a coherent organizational system.

Characteristics

- Persistent
- Structural
- Independent of language
- Independent of individual interactions
- Cannot be reduced to Organizational Objects
- Cannot be reduced to object properties

Validated Through

AD-002 – Organizational Relationship

### Knowledge Asset

Knowledge Assets preserve organizational knowledge.

Knowledge Assets guide organizational behavior, capability development and decision making.

### Work Object

Work Objects represent governed organizational work progressing through defined organizational state transitions.

---

## Relationship

Relationships define how organizational concepts are connected.

Relationships possess organizational meaning and are governed independently of implementation.

Relationships are structural rather than behavioral.

---

## Modes of Work

Modes of Work describe the fundamental forms of organizational activity.

Current candidate Modes of Work include:

- Know
- Think
- Do
- Improve

Modes classify organizational work.

They do not perform organizational work.

---

# Context Concepts

The following concepts are considered context concepts.

They interact with the organization but are not currently considered part of the Core Organizational Ontology.

## Stakeholders

Individuals or organizations possessing an interest in organizational outcomes.

Examples:

- Customers
- Patients
- Citizens
- Suppliers
- Shareholders

---

## Resources

Resources enable organizational work but are not themselves Organizational Objects.

Examples:

- Buildings
- Equipment
- Financial Assets
- Infrastructure
- Computing Resources

# Architectural Constraints

The following constraints apply.

- Every future HELIX Capability shall conform to this ontology.
- Every future Reference Model shall extend this ontology.
- Specifications shall not redefine concepts established by this ontology.
- Standards shall inherit terminology from this ontology.

## Organizational Governance

Status

Validated

Definition

The persistent organizational mechanism that legitimizes decision authority, constrains organizational behavior, and preserves organizational integrity throughout operation and change.

Responsibility

- Establishes legitimate decision authority.
- Preserves organizational integrity.
- Enables controlled organizational evolution.

Characteristics

- Persistent
- Independent of hierarchy
- Independent of management
- Independent of individual actors
- Cannot be reduced to Purpose
- Cannot be reduced to Organizational Relationships

Validated Through

AD-003 – Organizational Governance

## Organizational Learning

Status

Validated

Definition

The persistent organizational phenomenon through which previous Organizational Outcomes systematically influence future organizational behavior, structures, governance, or capabilities.

Responsibility

- Explains organizational evolution.
- Connects Outcomes with future organizational behavior.
- Enables continuous organizational improvement.
- Supports organizational adaptation through experience.

Characteristics

- Persistent
- Experience-driven
- Outcome-dependent
- Cannot be reduced to Governance
- Cannot be reduced to Capability
- Cannot be reduced to Organizational Change
- Cannot be reduced to Organizational Outcome

Validated Through

AD-005 – Organizational Learning
