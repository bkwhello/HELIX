- Engineering Case removal testing

- Falsification attempts

The investigation established that:

- Real-world application alone does not automatically produce usable validation evidence.
- An Engineering Case cannot be reduced to a narrative case study.
- A client engagement may contain an Engineering Case but is not inherently an Engineering Case.
- An Engineering Case must be capable of producing evidence against HELIX, not merely evidence consistent with it.
- Explicit case boundaries and observed conditions are necessary to preserve the meaning of case evidence.
- Supporting, contradictory, uncertain, unexplained, and failure evidence must remain visible.
- Bidirectional traceability is necessary for inspection and backward failure analysis.
- Independent reproduction is not an intrinsic requirement of an individual Engineering Case.
- An Engineering Case may test a bounded subset of the HELIX Engineering Lifecycle.
- An Engineering Case is not reducible to a document, container, or protocol.
- The Engineering Case is fundamentally the bounded engineering application itself.
- A failed or incomplete application may still produce valid and valuable case evidence.

No stronger competing explanation survived.

## Final Discovery

An Engineering Case is a bounded and disciplined application of the HELIX Organizational Engineering Process to organizational reality that preserves inspectable, traceable, and condition-aware evidence, including supporting, contradictory, uncertain, unexplained, and failure evidence, for subsequent validation and refinement of the discipline.

The Engineering Case is fundamentally the bounded application itself.

It is not reducible to:

- a case study,
- a client project,
- a demonstration,
- a documentary artifact,
- an evidence container,
- a protocol,
- or a validation conclusion.

A protocol may govern its execution.

A container may preserve its records.

Artifacts may make its engineering evidence inspectable and traceable.

None of these individually constitutes the Engineering Case itself.

An Engineering Case may cover a bounded subset of the HELIX Organizational Engineering Lifecycle, provided its scope, tested stages, observed conditions, and termination point remain explicit.

An Engineering Case does not require successful completion to produce valid case evidence.

Failures, contradictions, uncertainties, and unexplained organizational phenomena are legitimate engineering evidence.
