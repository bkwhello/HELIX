## Relationship Statement

Within the evidence each claim independently establishes, Konnichiwa's non-branded search and local-pack presence is markedly stronger for its two flagship dining formats (teppanyaki, omakase — OC-001's positions 4.47 and 4.70; OC-003's local-pack position 2 of 3) than for its broader categories (japans restaurant utrecht, sushi utrecht — OC-001's positions 7.4–8.1 and 14.76). Separately, and without evidence connecting the two patterns, a real, measurable entity-naming inconsistency exists (OC-004: "konichiwa" misspelling driving over 1,100 Search Console impressions and over 1,100 GBP profile-discovery searches).

## Organizational Meaning

Considered together, these three claims show that Konnichiwa's discovery problem, where one exists, is not uniform: it is concentrated in the broader, more contested categories, while the flagship formats already perform well by two independent measurement methods (organic and local-pack). The naming inconsistency is a distinct, separately-confirmed condition that coexists with this visibility pattern rather than being shown to explain it — a future Diagnosis inquiry into "why is japans restaurant utrecht/sushi utrecht weaker" should not assume naming is the cause without further evidence, and a future inquiry into "does naming inconsistency matter" should not assume the flagship-format strength disproves it.

## Confidence

- Level: Medium-High for the contrast pattern itself (grounded in two of this case's highest-reliability evidence classes — first-party Search Console export and a genuinely location-controlled local-pack observation); Low for treating the naming inconsistency as connected to the visibility contrast, since no evidence tested that connection
- Rationale: each constituent claim already carries its own confidence rating (High for OC-001's SC data, High for OC-003's single observation, High for OC-004's variant volume) — the relationship's confidence is capped by the weakest link, which is the absence of any evidence testing whether naming affects the contrast

## Limitations

- OC-003 is a single point/time/device — it cannot be generalized to "local-pack presence is strong" beyond that one observation, only cited as one corroborating data point for the omakase theme specifically.
- OC-001's "average position" limitation (does not capture sessions where Konnichiwa was entirely absent) applies unchanged within this relationship.
- No claim in this relationship measures whether the contrast (flagship vs. broad) is stable over time — all are single-window or single-point observations.
- E-03 (search-term composition trend) is Blocking for any claim that discovery composition shifted over time — this relationship makes no such claim.

## Excluded Interpretation

- That the naming inconsistency causes, contributes to, or explains the flagship/broad-category contrast.
- That the flagship-format strength disproves any effect of the naming inconsistency.
- That this pattern is stable across seasons, months, or beyond the stated windows.
- That organic ranking and local-pack ranking can be merged into one "visibility score" — they remain distinct Google surfaces throughout.
