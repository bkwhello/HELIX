#### Organizational Understanding

Konnichiwa's non-branded search and local-discovery presence is not uniform across its offerings, but where it is strong, it is genuinely strong and independently corroborated across two different Google surfaces (organic search and the local pack), for the two dining formats the organization treats as flagship (teppanyaki and omakase). This presence coexists with a longstanding, measurable entity-naming inconsistency that runs underneath all of it — search engines and profile-discovery systems already tolerate the inconsistency well enough to still surface Konnichiwa, but the inconsistency itself remains present and quantifiable, not merely historical.

For its broader, more generic dining categories ("Japans restaurant Utrecht," "sushi Utrecht"), presence is markedly weaker, and this case's evidence base does not yet explain why — only that it is real and measured (OC-001).

#### Synthesis of Organizational Claims

OC-001 establishes the ordinal pattern of search-position strength across four themes: strong for both flagship formats, weak for the two broader categories.

OC-003 independently corroborates the omakase reading specifically, via a different Google surface (local pack) and a different, more rigorous method (genuinely Utrecht-located, incognito observation) than OC-001's aggregate Search Console data. That two independent methods on two independent surfaces both indicate a favorable omakase position — where an earlier, less-controlled check had suggested weakness — increases confidence that the pattern described in OC-001 for omakase specifically reflects Konnichiwa's real position, not an artifact of Search Console's "average position" metric alone (a limitation OC-001 itself names).

OC-004 establishes a condition that runs orthogonally to OC-001/OC-003: naming is inconsistent across external representations, and this inconsistency has measurable real search volume behind it. Critically, OC-004 does not contradict OC-001/OC-003 — Konnichiwa's flagship-format presence is strong despite the naming inconsistency, not because the inconsistency has been resolved. This is itself informative: it suggests Google's own systems are already partially compensating for the inconsistency (autocorrect-style tolerance, as OC-004's Alternative Interpretations section notes), which is a different, more specific condition than "naming inconsistency is actively harming visibility" — that stronger claim is not established by this evidence.

Together, these three claims describe one coherent condition: Konnichiwa has a real, corroborated, format-specific search and local presence that exists *despite* an unresolved, quantifiable naming inconsistency, and *before* any explanation exists for why the broader/generic categories underperform relative to the flagship formats.

#### Understanding Boundaries

OU-001 establishes the shape and internal relationship of Konnichiwa's current search/entity presence across the claims named above. It does not establish:

- why "japans restaurant utrecht" and "sushi utrecht" underperform relative to teppanyaki/omakase (a Diagnosis question, not yet asked);
- that correcting the naming inconsistency would materially change search performance (no causal mechanism tested — OC-004's Causal Status is explicitly Descriptive/Associative);
- that the favorable omakase position (OC-003) generalizes beyond its single verified observation point/time/device;
- that this understanding connects to Google Business Profile performance (OC-002) or reservation outcomes (OC-007) — those remain separate, unintegrated claims (see decisions/DD-011);
- a required design intervention of any kind.
