#### Organizational Claim

Konnichiwa's business name is represented inconsistently across external channels — at least three variants are in active use ("Konnichiwa," "Konnichi Wa," and "Konichiwa"/"KONICHIWA") — and this inconsistency corresponds to measurable, non-trivial real search volume: 104 clicks / 4,813 impressions in Search Console and 1,130 Google Business Profile discovery-searches use the misspelled "konichiwa" form specifically.

#### Organizational Relevance

Entity consistency affects how reliably search engines and AI systems can associate all external mentions with the same organization (VD-006, Entity Ambiguity, understanding/EC-002-VD-taxonomy.md). Quantifying that this is not a theoretical risk but an active pattern with real search volume behind it gives the Marketing Visibility Capability a concrete, sized problem rather than an assumed one.

#### Scope

- Channel: Website (own site), TripAdvisor, TheFork, Instagram, Facebook, Eet.nu, Yelp (naming variants); Search Console and Google Business Profile (search-volume quantification)
- Query/page: Queries containing "konichiwa" (misspelled) specifically, plus the underlying entity-listing variants
- Geography: Not separately controlled — aggregate across both data sources
- Device: Not separately broken out for this specific finding
- Time period: 22–23 July 2026 (evidence collection dates); Search Console figures cover the same window as O-001

#### Evidential Basis

EV-001 (HV-IV-001) first identified "Konnichiwa" vs. "Konnichi Wa" as parallel forms across the website/TripAdvisor/TheFork versus Instagram/Facebook/Eet.nu. EV-004 (HV-IV-003) added a third variant, "Konichiwa"/"KONICHIWA" (single n), found on Yelp and in a Tripadvisor page title. EV-014 (Search Console, O-001.md) then quantified real search behavior using the misspelled form: 104 clicks and 4,813 impressions across the covered window, at a reasonable average position (3.99 for "konichiwa" alone), meaning Google already serves Konnichiwa's own results for this misspelling — the inconsistency has not fully broken discovery, but it is measurably present in real user queries. EV-015 (GBP, O-002.md) independently corroborates via a second, unrelated data source: "konichiwa" (misspelled) drives 1,130 of the profile's discovery-searches, and the full-address variant "konnichiwa, mariaplaats, utrecht" (1,639) suggests some searchers compensate for naming uncertainty by adding the address.

#### Confidence

- Level: High for the existence of naming variants and for the two independent, quantified search-volume figures; Medium for treating the two search-volume figures as fully independent confirmation, since the same underlying misspelling drives both
- Rationale: Two different platforms (Search Console, Google Business Profile) both report substantial volume against the same misspelling, using different collection methods — this reduces (but does not eliminate) the chance that either figure is a measurement artifact specific to one platform.

#### Limitations

- No causal link is established between the naming inconsistency and any specific business outcome (e.g., lost reservations, reduced visibility) — this claim only establishes that the inconsistency exists and that non-trivial search volume interacts with it.
- The three-variant inventory (Konnichiwa / Konnichi Wa / Konichiwa) may not be exhaustive — only the platforms actually checked in HV-IV-001/003 were inventoried.
- It is not established whether the misspelled-query searchers ultimately found and selected Konnichiwa, or whether some fraction did not (no completed-action data tied to these specific queries).

#### Alternative Interpretations

- The 4,813 impressions against "konichiwa" could partly reflect Google's own autocorrect/fuzzy-matching behavior (Google may show correct-spelling results for a misspelled query regardless of any external naming inconsistency) rather than the inconsistency itself driving the volume — this would mean the search-volume figure reflects general misspelling-tolerance in search engines generally, not something Konnichiwa's own listings caused. Not adjudicated here.
- The full-address query pattern ("konnichiwa, mariaplaats, utrecht") could reflect normal navigational search behavior unrelated to naming confusion (some searchers always include an address). Not adjudicated here.

#### Causal Status

Descriptive/Associative. The claim describes co-occurrence (naming variants exist; real search volume exists against a non-canonical form) without asserting that one causes the other's magnitude.

#### Falsification Tests

1. **Is this a single measurement misrepresented as a trend?** No — two independent platforms, both showing volume over their respective measurement windows, not a single-session read.
2. **Is the claim broader than its measurement scope?** Checked — the claim is scoped to the specific misspelling "konichiwa" and its measured volume; it does not claim the inconsistency affects all queries or all platforms equally.
3. **Could seasonality explain the condition?** Not applicable — naming variants are a structural/listing property, not a seasonal search-behavior pattern; the volume figures are simple counts over their windows, not trend claims.
4. **Could branded demand distort the interpretation?** This claim is explicitly about a branded-adjacent query (the business's own misspelled name) — branding is the subject of the claim, not a distorting factor to control for.
5. **Could device, location, or personalization affect the result?** Both source figures (Search Console, GBP) are aggregate account-level data, not personalized single-session reads — lower risk here than for O-003.
6. **Is unavailable data being interpreted as poor performance?** No — both figures are present, real counts.
7. **Does conflicting evidence exist?** None found.
8. **Would a reasonable challenger accept the confidence level?** Yes, with the Alternative Interpretations (autocorrect, navigational search) explicitly preserved rather than dismissed.

Outcome: **Survives with Narrowing** (Causal Status downgraded from an initial-draft implication of "the inconsistency drives confusion" to explicit Descriptive/Associative framing; Alternative Interpretations section added to acknowledge Google autocorrect as a live alternative explanation for part of the volume).

#### Boundaries

OC-004 establishes that naming variants exist and that measurable search volume interacts with one of them. It does not establish:

- that the inconsistency causes any specific negative outcome (lost customers, reduced conversion);
- that correcting the naming would change search volume or behavior;
- the relative size of the problem compared to other findings in this case (e.g., the GBP decline in OC-002 is measured independently and not compared here);
- which platforms, beyond those checked, may also show variant naming.

#### Contradictory Evidence

None currently recorded.

OC-004 is sufficiently supported by EV-001, EV-004, EV-014, and EV-015 for use in subsequent Organizational Understanding, with explicit preservation of the Google-autocorrect alternative explanation.
