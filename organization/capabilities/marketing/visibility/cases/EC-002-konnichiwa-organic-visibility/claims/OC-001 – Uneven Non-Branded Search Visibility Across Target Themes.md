#### Organizational Claim

Within the 21 Apr–21 Jun 2026 Search Console window, Konnichiwa's non-branded organic search visibility is uneven across its four target themes: strong for "teppanyaki utrecht" (average position 4.47) and "omakase utrecht" (average position 4.70), materially weaker for "japans restaurant utrecht" (average position 7.4–8.1 depending on phrasing), and weak for "sushi utrecht" (average position 14.76).

#### Organizational Relevance

The Marketing Visibility Capability's core outcome is discoverability for high-intent local search themes. An uneven baseline across the four themes named in Purpose.md means any future prioritization decision (which theme to invest in first) has a real, evidence-backed starting point rather than an assumed one.

#### Scope

- Channel: Google organic search (Search Console)
- Query/page: "teppanyaki utrecht," "omakase utrecht," "japans restaurant utrecht" / "japanese restaurant utrecht," "sushi utrecht"
- Geography: Not device-location-controlled at the Search Console level (aggregate, predominantly Dutch traffic per O-001's geography note — 94% Netherlands)
- Device: All devices combined (Search Console default); O-001 separately shows mobile averages better than desktop across all queries combined (6.99 vs. 12.91), not broken out per theme
- Time period: 21 Apr–21 Jun 2026 (61-day daily window within a "last 3 months" filter)

#### Evidential Basis

EV-014 establishes exact average positions and click/impression counts for all four themes over the covered window, directly from Konnichiwa's own Search Console property — not a simulated or personalized search. EV-018 (O-003) independently corroborates the omakase reading specifically, via a different method (Utrecht-located local-pack observation) and a different Google surface (local pack, not organic) — the two do not measure the same thing, but neither contradicts the other, which is itself informative: two independent methods, on two different surfaces, both indicate a stronger-than-assumed omakase position, whereas the case's earlier informal, non-geo-controlled checks (evidence/HV-IV-003.md) had suggested "weak." Together, EV-014 alone is sufficient to establish the ordinal pattern (teppanyaki ≈ omakase > japans restaurant > sushi); EV-018 adds independent, cross-surface support specifically for the omakase reading.

#### Confidence

- Level: High for the Search Console figures themselves; Medium for treating "average position" as a complete visibility measure (see Alternative Interpretations)
- Rationale: Search Console is a first-party, non-personalized aggregate over real search traffic — the strongest evidence class available to this case. The reduction to a single "average position" number per query is a known simplification (see below).

#### Limitations

- Search Console's "average position" is computed only over sessions where Konnichiwa's own pages actually appeared in results — it does not capture sessions where Konnichiwa was entirely absent while a competitor appeared instead. A theme could have a good average position and still lose most of the underlying demand to competitors who out-impress it.
- The 61-day daily window is not the same as the "last 3 months" filter Search Console reports its totals against — a reporting-lag artifact, not a data error, but it means the very latest weeks are not reflected.
- Query-level totals in this export are capped and undercount true totals (616 vs. 906 clicks) — the four named themes are large enough queries to be unaffected by the cap, but the broader query universe is not fully visible.
- No device-level or day-of-week breakdown exists per theme (only in aggregate).

#### Alternative Interpretations

- The apparent "omakase strength" could be an artifact of low absolute volume (29 clicks, 388 impressions) rather than genuine prominence — a small, favorable sample is more volatile than teppanyaki's similarly small but slightly larger sample (32 clicks, 375 impressions). Both themes have low absolute volume; the claim is about relative ordering within this baseline, not about volume-independent certainty.
- "Japans restaurant utrecht"'s weaker position could reflect genuine competitive crowding (a broader, more contested category) rather than any Konnichiwa-specific weakness — evidence/HV-IV-006.md's competitor register is consistent with this (multiple general Japanese/Asian competitors identified), but this claim does not adjudicate between "crowded category" and "Konnichiwa-specific weakness" as the explanation — that is a Diagnosis question, not covered here.

#### Causal Status

Descriptive.

#### Falsification Tests

1. **Is this a single measurement misrepresented as a stable trend?** Rejected as a defect — Search Console's figures are aggregated over a 61-day window, not a single-session snapshot; O-003's cross-check is a single point-in-time, and the claim's wording ("within the 21 Apr–21 Jun 2026 window") does not extend O-003's single observation into a trend claim about the local pack.
2. **Is the claim broader than its measurement scope?** Narrowing applied: the original draft did not specify device/geography scope explicitly; added above.
3. **Could seasonality explain the pattern?** Not tested — no prior-year or prior-period comparison exists for these specific queries. The claim describes the pattern within the stated window only, not a seasonally adjusted or generalized ranking.
4. **Could branded demand distort this?** No — all four themes are explicitly non-branded (none contain "Konnichiwa"), so this risk does not apply to this claim.
5. **Could device, location, or personalization affect the result?** Yes, materially, for O-003's corroborating local-pack data point (single device, single location, single time) — already reflected in Scope and Limitations; does not affect the primary Search Console figures, which are Konnichiwa's own aggregate account data, not a personalized session.
6. **Is unavailable data being interpreted as poor performance?** No — all four themes have real, present data; none are treated as absent.
7. **Does conflicting evidence exist?** Yes — evidence/HV-IV-003.md's informal, non-geo-controlled read of "omakase Utrecht" as "weak" nominally conflicts with the Search Console position. This is addressed directly in Alternative Interpretations and is why CR-005 exists; it does not invalidate the Search Console data itself, which remains the higher-reliability source per this case's own evidence hierarchy (HV-MP-001 explicitly ranks a single personalized/informal search below platform-export data).
8. **Would a reasonable challenger accept the stated confidence level?** Yes, with the explicit caveat about "average position" already added under Limitations.

Outcome: **Survives with Narrowing** (explicit scope fields added; average-position limitation made explicit).

#### Boundaries

OC-001 establishes the ordinal pattern of non-branded search visibility across four specific queries, within one 61-day Search Console window. It does not establish:

- that this pattern is stable over months or seasons;
- that any specific intervention would change it;
- why "sushi utrecht" underperforms (a Diagnosis question);
- device- or location-specific rankings for teppanyaki, japans restaurant, or sushi (only omakase has a corroborating device/location-specific check);
- that "average position" fully captures visibility (see Limitations).

#### Contradictory Evidence

evidence/HV-IV-003.md's informal, non-location-controlled read of "omakase Utrecht" as weak. Preserved, not deleted; addressed in Alternative Interpretations and via CR-005 (Resolved for Initial Baseline — see Challenge Evidence/CR-register.md).

OC-001 is sufficiently supported by EV-014 (primary) and EV-018 (corroborating, cross-surface) for use in subsequent Organizational Understanding. The claim remains bounded by its stated Scope and Limitations. No Organizational Diagnosis established from this claim alone.
