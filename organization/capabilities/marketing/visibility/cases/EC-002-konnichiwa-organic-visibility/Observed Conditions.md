# Observed Conditions
---

The conditions under which case evidence is produced, per AD-014.

- Organization type: Independent Japanese restaurant
- Organization: Konnichiwa
- Geography: Utrecht, Netherlands
- Engineer familiarity with organization: Very high (Engineering Lead is the owner)
- Engineer relationship to organization: Direct organizational actor with deep operational and historical knowledge
- Investigation independence: Limited — the Engineering Lead has direct organizational involvement
- Evidence accessibility: Mixed — direct organizational facts (Organizational Reality Register, HV-IV-002) are highly accessible; platform-level data (Search Console, GA4, Google Business Profile, Guestplan exports) requires an export the AI agent cannot self-collect (no account access)
- Prior evidence: Substantial evidence already exists from a prior, broader case-establishment effort dated 22 July 2026 (HV-IV-001 through HV-IV-007). This evidence is preserved and reused; the case-definition framing that originally produced it is not (see Case Identity.md, Note on case history)
- Known validation risk: Prior knowledge may be unconsciously treated as evidence without explicit observation or substantiation
- Known validation risk: AI-collected search and AI-system test results (HV-IV-003, HV-IV-004) may diverge from what an individual user sees, due to personalization, location, and timing
- Known validation advantage: Deep access to operational reality, historical context, and direct owner confirmation for organizational facts
- Organization scale: Small enterprise — no dedicated marketing or visibility team; capability must remain operable by the owner alone

Prior knowledge shall not automatically be treated as evidence. Statements based on prior knowledge must either be explicitly recorded as unverified input, be supported by evidence, or remain classified as uncertainty (see `observations/` classification per item).

Observed conditions may be refined as the case progresses. Changes shall be preserved rather than silently overwritten where they affect the interpretation of evidence.
