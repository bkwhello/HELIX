# HV-MP-001 – HELIX Visibility Measurement Plan

## Status

Proposed

## Case

EC-002 – Engineering Organizational Visibility of Konnichiwa

## Discipline

HELIX Visibility

## Edition

Small Enterprise Edition

## Organization

Konnichiwa

## Purpose

This measurement plan defines how EC-002 will collect, preserve, compare, and interpret data to determine whether HELIX Visibility is producing measurable improvement for Konnichiwa.

The plan must establish:

1. Konnichiwa’s visibility state before implementation.
2. Which interventions are performed.
3. Which measurable changes occur after implementation.
4. Whether those changes can reasonably be connected to the intervention.
5. Whether visibility improvements lead to useful customer and business outcomes.
6. Whether the improvements remain stable over time.
7. Whether the HELIX Visibility method is effective, maintainable, and reusable.

---

# 1. Measurement Objective

The measurement objective is:

> Determine whether HELIX Visibility causes Konnichiwa to become more accurately represented, more easily discovered, better understood, more frequently recommended, and more often selected for approved customer intents.

Success must not be measured through traffic alone.

HELIX Visibility must measure the complete chain:

```text
Organizational Reality
        ↓
External Representation
        ↓
Search and AI Understanding
        ↓
Discovery
        ↓
Customer Action
        ↓
Reservation or Business Outcome
```

A visibility action is successful only when it produces a verified improvement somewhere in this chain.

---

# 2. Measurement Principles

## HV-MP-P-001 – Baseline Before Intervention

No major implementation may begin before the relevant baseline has been recorded.

Without a baseline, improvement cannot be demonstrated.

---

## HV-MP-P-002 – Intent-Specific Measurement

Visibility must be measured for defined customer intents.

General visibility scores may support analysis but may not replace intent-level evidence.

---

## HV-MP-P-003 – Action Traceability

Every material website, content, schema, profile, review, listing, technical, or knowledge change must be connected to:

* A visibility defect.
* An approved target intent.
* An expected result.
* A measurement method.
* A validation date.
* An implementation record.

---

## HV-MP-P-004 – Separate Leading and Business Indicators

HELIX must distinguish between:

### Leading indicators

Signals that machine understanding and discoverability are improving.

Examples:

* Increased indexed coverage.
* Better query positions.
* More accurate AI answers.
* More complete business information.
* More relevant impressions.
* Increased recommendation frequency.

### Business indicators

Signals that improved visibility is producing organizational value.

Examples:

* Reservation clicks.
* Calls.
* Direction requests.
* Completed reservations.
* Higher-value bookings.
* Private dining enquiries.
* Workshop bookings.
* Gift-card purchases.

A leading indicator is not automatically a business outcome.

---

## HV-MP-P-005 – Small Enterprise Proportionality

The measurement system must be maintainable without a dedicated analytics team.

It must prioritize:

* Automated collection.
* A limited number of useful indicators.
* Clear decision rules.
* Monthly owner-level reporting.
* Minimal manual administration.
* Evidence preservation where it matters.

---

## HV-MP-P-006 – No False Attribution

HELIX must not claim that an intervention caused a result when other plausible causes have not been considered.

Possible external influences include:

* Seasonality.
* Holidays.
* Weather.
* Events in Utrecht.
* Advertising.
* Social-media activity.
* Menu changes.
* Price changes.
* Review spikes.
* Competitor closures.
* Google or AI-system changes.

Where direct causation cannot be proven, the result must be described as an observed association.

| Indicator                            | Weight | Purpose                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | -----: | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Reality and Representation Integrity |    20% | Measures factual correctness and external coverage |

| Search Visibility                    |    20% | Measures discovery through search and maps         |
| AI Understanding                     |    20% | Measures machine understanding and recommendation  |
| Customer Action                      |    15% | Measures useful customer behaviour                 |
| Business Outcome                     |    10% | Measures organizational value                      |

The initial total score may be calculated as:

```text
HELIX Visibility Score =
Integrity × 0.20
+ Consistency × 0.15
+ Search Visibility × 0.20
+ AI Understanding × 0.20
+ Customer Action × 0.15
+ Business Outcome × 0.10
```

The score is a management indicator.

It must never replace the underlying evidence.

# 9. AI Visibility Test Protocol

## Test Set

A fixed test set must be created.

Initial minimum:

* 5 identity intents.
* 10 commercial intents.
* 5 occasion intents.
* 5 experience intents.
* 5 informational intents.

Total initial test set:

**30 test scenarios**

## Test controls

Each test should record:

* Exact prompt.
* AI system.
* Model or product version where visible.
* Date.
* Language.
* Location context.
* Logged-in or logged-out state where relevant.
* Whether browsing or web search was active.
* Answer.
* Sources cited.
* Konnichiwa mention status.
* Competitors mentioned.
* Evaluation score.

## Test frequency

* Full baseline before implementation.
* Targeted test after each major intervention.
* Complete test set monthly during the first six months.
* Quarterly after the system becomes stable.

## Repetition

Because AI answers are probabilistic, important prompts should be repeated multiple times.

Recommended:

* Three runs per critical prompt.
* One run per lower-priority monitoring prompt.

Recommendation Frequency is calculated as:

```text
Number of runs recommending Konnichiwa
─────────────────────────────────────── × 100
Total number of valid runs
```

## AI Factual Accuracy Score

Because a factual answer is rarely simply right or wrong, each scored run must be classified into exactly one of three states before scoring:

* **Fully correct** — every tested fact in the answer matches the validated Organizational Reality.
* **Partially correct** — the answer identifies Konnichiwa correctly but at least one tested fact is wrong, outdated, or missing.
* **Incorrect** — the answer misidentifies Konnichiwa, or the tested facts are substantially wrong.

Each state carries a fixed point value:

```text
Fully correct     = 100 points
Partially correct =  50 points
Incorrect         =   0 points
```

The AI Factual Accuracy Score is the mean across all valid runs in the scored round:

```text
Sum of points across all valid runs
──────────────────────────────────── 
Number of valid runs
```

This score must always be reported alongside, not instead of, the raw state counts (e.g. "0 fully correct, 2 partially correct, 2 incorrect — score 25/100"). A single aggregate percentage without the underlying counts is not sufficient evidence under HV-MP-P-006 (No False Attribution) — a 25/100 driven by "all partial" and a 25/100 driven by "half perfect, half wrong" describe different problems and require different interventions.

A round is also scoped to whichever test scenarios were actually run. A score computed from one scenario (e.g. "opening hours") must be labeled with that scope (e.g. "1 of 30 planned scenarios") and must not be presented as a general AI Understanding score until enough of the approved test set has been run.

# 12. Measurement Windows

Each intervention must be evaluated at multiple points.

## Immediate validation

Within 1–7 days:

* Was the implementation published correctly?
* Is the page accessible?
* Is tracking functioning?
* Is structured data valid?
* Are internal links working?
* Are facts correct?

## Early result

After approximately 28 days:

* Has indexing occurred?
* Are impressions appearing?
* Is machine understanding changing?
* Are new defects visible?

## Development result

After approximately 56 days:

* Is the direction of change becoming clearer?
* Are qualified clicks or actions increasing?
* Are AI recommendations changing?

## Outcome result

After approximately 90 days:

* Has the intervention produced a meaningful improvement?
* Is there a customer or business impact?
* Should the asset be maintained, improved, consolidated, or retired?

Some interventions may require longer measurement because visibility systems update at different speeds.

# 13. Success Thresholds

## Intervention-level verdicts

### Earned

The expected improvement occurred, the evidence is sufficiently strong, and no major negative effect was identified.

### Provisionally Earned

A positive change is visible, but the measurement period or evidence is not yet sufficient.

### Inconclusive

No reliable conclusion can be drawn.

### Not Earned

The expected improvement did not occur.

### Harmful

The intervention caused or is strongly associated with a meaningful negative effect.

* Page title.
* Main copy.
* internal links.

Each experiment should include:

* Hypothesis.
* Baseline.
* Intervention.
* Expected signal.
* Observation period.
* Confounding factors.
* Result.

# 22. Success Definition

HELIX Visibility is successful when preserved evidence demonstrates that:

1. Konnichiwa is represented more accurately.
2. Important sources are more consistent.
3. Relevant search visibility increases.
4. AI systems understand Konnichiwa more accurately.
5. Konnichiwa is recommended more often for approved intents.
6. More qualified customers take reservation or contact actions.
7. Relevant business outcomes improve.
8. Improvements can be connected to recorded interventions.
9. The capability remains maintainable by a small enterprise.
10. The method produces reusable engineering knowledge for future HELIX cases.

---

# 23. Failure Definition

HELIX Visibility has not proven success when:

* More content exists but understanding does not improve.
* Rankings increase for irrelevant searches.
* Traffic increases without qualified actions.
* AI mentions increase but factual accuracy decreases.
* Visibility work requires excessive manual effort.
* Interventions cannot be traced.
* Results cannot be distinguished from seasonality or unrelated activity.
* The system produces reports but no useful decisions.
* The method cannot identify which actions worked.
* Konnichiwa cannot maintain the designed capability.

---

# 24. Foundational Measurement Rule

> HELIX Visibility may not claim success because work was completed.

> Success is earned only when measured visibility, understanding, recommendation, customer action, or business value improves against a preserved baseline.

# 25. Measurement Declaration

HV-MP-001 establishes the measurement system required to prove or disprove the effectiveness of HELIX Visibility.

The measurement system will evaluate both:

* The external visibility improvement of Konnichiwa.
* The engineering effectiveness of the HELIX Visibility method.

No visibility intervention will be considered successful solely because it was implemented.

Every material result must be observed, compared, interpreted, challenged, and preserved.
